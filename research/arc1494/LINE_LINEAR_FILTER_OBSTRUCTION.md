# L-meta-1494: line-linear filter obstruction

Status: partial mathematical result, not a proof of L-meta-1494. No lease or upstream submission for this step. Scope: circuits satisfying the L-meta-1494 arc promise and having no AND gate with two syntactically line-dependent predecessors, after removing dead gates and normalizing constants and unary gates. The circuit-to-transposition bridge and the counting argument are mathematical proofs, not yet end-to-end Lean theorems. The finite charge and geometry lemmas are separately checked by Lean, as recorded below.

## 1. Stronger line-OR charge: 37 OR gates

Use the previous line-OR gate bound: each of 13 output lines L and 54 disjoint four-arcs A must accumulate three false-wire OR merge events. Additionally there must be at least one false OR gate whose singleton support contains the entire line L. Indeed, choose the first false wire with this property in topological order, which exists because the output is such a wire. It cannot be an input or a constant (constant true is not false). It cannot be an AND, because some parent is false and contains the output's singleton support. Nor can it be a unary gate. Thus it is a binary OR. Call this a completion event.

For an OR with singleton supports U,V, write S=U union V and W_L(S)=number of four-arcs disjoint from L and S. Charge 10 per merge event and 1 per completion event. A gate's completion count is at most sum W_L(S) over L contained in S, and its merge count is at most the earlier disjoint-partition bound. Assign overlapping points of U,V arbitrarily to one side: any actual merge has nonempty disjoint local supports on L, so this cannot remove any counted merge. Completion capacity is unchanged. Direct exact enumeration of all 8192 masks, coarse bounds for 7139, and 7371 explicit unordered bipartitions in the remaining masks verifies that 10 times merge capacity plus completion capacity is at most 600. No probabilistic or solver assumption. This is independently checked in Arc1494LineOrCharge.lean, all_completion_representatives_pass, using 630 cyclic representatives (translations preserve geometry).

Demand = 13*54*(3*10+1) = 21762, exceeding 36*600=21600. Consequently any monotone multioutput circuit computing all 13 line disjunctions on arcs has at least 37 OR gates, independent of AND count. This strengthens the previous 36-OR bound. The prior total gate bound of 39 remains valid. Moreover the dirty-line argument of the previous proof yields at least 39 ORs whenever there are at most two AND gates with singleton support of size at least two, regardless of how many other ANDs there are.

## 2. Transposition of a line-linear incidence circuit

Take a normalized line-linear circuit, where a wire is line-dependent if its syntactic ancestor cone contains a y input. Every line-dependent AND has at most one line-dependent input. For a fixed point assignment x, each such wire computes an affine OR of y variables: a constant contribution plus a union of terms y_w AND a coefficient depending only on x. Distribute AND by a point-only wire over those terms. The output's constant contribution is false on every arc (y=0). Erase all point-only branches entering line-dependent OR gates, preserving its line-linear part; erase resulting unary gates and dead gates. This is sound on the promise, including all incident pairs. Normalize any constants/unary inputs as usual. Every y input remains essential.

The resulting mixed DAG has 13 source y wires, f filter gates (one mixed predecessor, multiplied by a pure point function h_i), j two-mixed-parent OR joins, and one output. Retain its pure-point subcircuit of o OR and a AND gates, with total o+a+f+j <= original size. All mixed vertices are ancestors of the output, so each nonroot vertex has >=1 outgoing edge (count occurrences, permitting repeated predecessors). Hence j>=12 from f+2j >= 13+f+j-1.

Reverse the mixed edges. At the original root use coefficient true. At each other mixed vertex take the OR of the contributions from its outgoing edges; an outgoing edge into an original filter gate is weighted by that filter's pure point value, otherwise weight true. This reverse computation yields at each y_w the coefficient t_w(x), equal to the original output on the one-hot line assignment w. The scalar arc promise and monotonicity imply t_w is precisely OR(L_w) on every arc. Reverse OR count is at most sum(outdegree(v)-1) = j-12 over all nonroot vertices. Reverse ANDs use at most one gate per original filter. A coefficient true may allow omission of an AND, but that is not needed here. Constant true can be propagated through the first reverse edges without extra OR gates. Therefore the encoder for t_w uses at most o+j-12 OR gates, counting pure-point ORs as well. At most a+f of its ANDs can have large singleton supports; each reverse AND has singleton support contained in the support of its h_i.

The f filters constitute a pure point interface to the mixed network. The separator-charge theorem gives f>=13. For a hypothetical <=63-gate circuit, 37 <= o+j-12, so f+a <=14. Thus f is 13 or 14. If f=13, a<=1; if f=14, a=0. This arithmetic is kernel-checked in linear_filter_range.

At f=13, previous rigidity shows the singleton supports are exactly the 13 singletons or the 13 lines. In the singleton case each reverse filter-AND has singleton support of size <=1. At most one pure AND has larger support, so the dirty-line argument gives 39 OR gates in the reverse encoder, i.e. o+j>=51; adding f=13 gives >=64. In the line case the pure-point encoder computes all 13 line disjunctions on arcs by the minimum-interface rigidity result; costs >=39 gates, and j>=12, f=13 give >=64. Therefore f=14, a=0 is necessary for a <=63-gate line-linear circuit.

For f=14, any support family containing all 13 singletons is ruled out the same way (at most one remaining fat reverse AND, no pure AND). A family containing all 13 lines is also ruled out: pure-point gates are OR-only, so those line supports are exact line ORs, costing 39 pure ORs; adding 14 filters and 12 joins gives at least 65. But not every 14-support separator cover contains one canonical family: replacing line mask 523 by two pieces 3 and 520 yields a 14-cover, with both canonical families absent. Exact finite certificate verifies this. A prior bounded SAT run for a noncanonical 14-cover returned UNKNOWN at 50010 conflicts; it was not a proof and is superseded by this explicit cover.

## 3. Geometry: exclusion of up to three non-line-contained filter supports

All facts here refer to singleton supports of the f=14 filters. A nonempty support is line-contained if it is a subset of a geometric line. A nonsingleton line-contained support belongs to a unique line. Empty supports and duplicate singleton supports do not help the separator requirement; if discarding one leaves <=13 effective supports then the earlier width/equality theorem forces a canonical 13-family, already excluded.

For each p with no singleton filter and each line L through p, call (p,L) missing if no nonsingleton support contained in L contains p. If no missing pairs exist, the line-contained supports alone are a valid separator cover: for any four-arc A excluding p, some line through p is disjoint from A, and the support inside that line is disjoint from A. A singleton filter suffices for points possessing one.

The following exact facts are independently checked in arc1494_line_contained_filters.py. Arc1494FilterGeometry.lean checks the finite facts (by cyclic translation of its four base point-line pairs, where applicable). For a point p on line L, let a good four-arc be one disjoint from L that hits the other three lines through p. There are 27 per pair. Every arc of <=3 points disjoint from L extends to some good four-arc. Exact checks: 6136 extensions, including 3744 noncollinear triple extensions, for all 52 incident pairs. Every nonempty point subset M of size k<=11 hits at least k+2 lines, and sizes 12 or 13 hit all 13. Verified all 8192 masks. The min neighborhood sizes for k=0..13 are [0,4,7,9,10,11,11,12,12,12,13,13,13,13].

### Zero non-line-contained supports

Let M be points with no singleton filter, k=|M|. Missing pairs cannot exist: any good four-arc would hit every other line through p, but to defeat every line-contained support on another line one needs a more careful choice. A good arc hitting a different line need not hit every subset of that line. HOWEVER choose the line-specific separator conclusion differently: on a good arc, it may avoid a proper subset of another line. Therefore the assertion that all four required line-specific supports must exist is NOT justified merely by hitting the other lines. The arguments in sections 3-4 below must use the stronger actual condition: an arc simultaneously hitting every other filter support containing p, not just every other geometric line. This is a critical gap. Do not treat the claimed exclusions of 0,1,2,3 non-line-contained supports as proved on the basis of the extension lemma alone. A rigorous proof of these exclusions remains open; the verified extension facts and neighborhood counts are retained as building blocks. The f=13 exclusion and f=14,a=0 restriction in section 2 are independent of this gap and remain valid.

## 4. Next obligation

For f=14 and OR-only pure encoders, price the reverse coefficient circuit using both support family size and actual OR-construction cost. The line-specific separator shortcut fails because proper subsets may avoid an arc even if their containing line meets it. Need a proof that either 39 pure+reverse ORs are necessary, or f=14 impossible under the exact support cover and mixed DAG constraints. Alternatively find a genuine <=63-gate witness. Nonlinear ANDs with two line-dependent parents are not covered by this route. No upstream claim on L-meta-1494.
