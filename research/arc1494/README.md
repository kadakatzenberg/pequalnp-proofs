## Stronger pure-side cut: a theorem via exact finite integer enumeration

Suppose an AND/OR circuit correct on the promised arc domain computes k point-only signals h_j(A) and subsequently uses only those signals and the original line variables in a monotone decoder. No point signal bypasses this interface. For each bit j let B_j={p:h_j({p})=true} be its singleton support. For p outside any arc A, choose w through p disjoint from A by recovery. Then the decoder is true at ({p}, {w}) and false at (A,{w}). Monotonicity in its bit arguments forces at least one j with h_j({p})=true and h_j(A)=false. Monotonicity of h_j implies B_j contains p and is disjoint from A. In particular, the family of all B_j containing p must cover the 162 four-arcs excluding p, where support B covers an arc precisely when they are disjoint.

For p=0 let the charge of this family be sum_j 1/|B_j|. The exact certificate arc1494_separator_charge.py proves:
- Charge >=1.
- Equality only for the singleton support {0}, or the pencil of the four geometric lines through 0, whose masks are 177,523,3137,4357.
By cyclic translations these statements hold at every point.

Completeness of the integer enumeration: singleton costs 1 and suffices; size >=10 covers none of the four-arcs (discard it), and duplicates may be discarded. Thus a below-one solution uses only distinct supports of sizes 2..9 with exact cost sum of 2520/size <2520. For each size all supports containing 0 are enumerated from 13-bit masks and their 162-row coverage sets are computed exactly. Max coverage by size 2..9 is [108,72,54,30,15,9,3,1]. Enumerate all multisets of support sizes with cost <=2520. Only 37 below-one profiles and 9 nonsingleton equality profiles have summed maximum coverage >=162. For every such profile, exhaustive backtracking visits supports in sorted order; prune only when uncovered rows exceed the sum of remaining per-size maxima, or when the next support's total coverage is less than the uncovered deficit. Exact integers and bitsets, no numerical tolerance, solver or heuristic cutoff. Summing the local charges over all 13 points yields at least 13. Each nonempty support B_j contributes exactly 1 across its |B_j| points, so at least 13 interface wires are required. Empty supports do not help. This is a rigorous mathematical reduction and exact integer check, not yet a complete Lean theorem. It is stronger than the information-theoretic 10-bit lower bound. If only a point subset S is encoded, augment its interface by the individual identity wires of all other points; therefore its interface must have at least |S| bits. The line-side statement follows by duality, testing only line arcs (which are allowed in the original problem).

## Rigidity at exactly 13 bits

All 13 nonempty support columns must be used and every point has charge exactly one. If some point has the pencil case, each point on any of its four lines belongs to a size-four support, so cannot have singleton-only charge. The pencil covers all 13 points. Hence every point has the pencil case, and the support family is exactly the 13 geometric lines. Otherwise all points have singleton-only charge, so the support family is the 13 singleton supports. Furthermore the actual functions on arcs are fixed: singleton bits are forced to be the corresponding input, as each absent point needs its sole separator off. In the pencil case, for each line L and each arc A disjoint from L, there exists a four-arc B extending A, disjoint from L, and a point p on L for which all other lines through p meet B. That property is exhaustively checked for every line and arc by the integer certificate: 54 qualifying four-arcs per line and zero missing extensions. Thus L is the only possible separator bit for p at B, so h_L(B)=false and by monotonicity h_L(A)=false. If A meets L, h_L(A)=true by singleton support and monotonicity. Therefore a minimum-width point-side interface is precisely the identity map or the line-OR map, up to output permutation, on the arc promise.

This theorem restricts arbitrary monotone pure-side signals, not just OR-only encoders. It does NOT rule out interleaved mixed circuits, interfaces of greater width, or prove total gate count 64.
